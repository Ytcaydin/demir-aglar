## Full description (max 4000)
Start in 1856 with the İzmir–Aydın line, the first railway in Anatolia, and build Turkey's rail network all the way to 2026.

Rails of Anatolia (Turkish title: "Demir Ağlar") is an offline 3D railway strategy game. Lay track, buy trains and carry the goods each city needs to grow it from a village into a metropolis.

LIVE THROUGH HISTORY
• Start in the Ottoman era: the İzmir–Aydın concession of 1856, the opening in 1866, the Suez Canal, the Ottoman Public Debt Administration, the Anatolian and Baghdad Railways
• Move into the Republic proclaimed on 29 October 1923, nationalize the railways and enter the State Railways era
• Or start directly in 1927
• Answer real historical events on decision cards

FOUR ERAS, FIVE TRAINS
• Steam, Diesel, Blue Train, Electric and YHT high-speed trains
• The map, roads, vehicles and skies change with every era: first planes, airports, cars on the roads
• Rain, snow and fog

GROW YOUR CITIES
• Connect the cities of Anatolia, each with its own needs and products
• Watch towns turn into metropolises as buildings rise in front of you
• Landmarks such as Ephesus, Troy, Cappadocia, Mount Nemrut, Pamukkale, Sumela Monastery and the Çanakkale Martyrs' Memorial appear in the year they opened to visitors; tap them to read a short introduction

RUN YOUR COMPANY
• Maintenance and breakdowns: keep your trains in shape
• Staff, loans, shares, orders and megaprojects
• Compete with a rival company, or buy out its lines
• Achievements, records and a city album
• New Generation: hand your company to the next generation and earn lasting perks
• Scenarios, including three Ottoman-era challenges

A FEAST FOR THE EYES
• Take shots of your network in photo mode and share them
• Fullscreen mode, a clean interface and an opening flyover

SHORT, RELAXED SESSIONS
• Build your network, close the app, and see what you earned when you come back
• No account, no login, no internet needed; progress stays on your device and can be backed up with a code
• Available in English and Turkish
